# Medlar Village: the players' brief

The persona's battle layer for each side (docs/player-prompt.md), built by tools/build_pack.py from tools/players_template.md and each army's tools/army_<side>.md, so that the two sections share the ground, the orders, the verdict and the clock word for word and differ in the two armies. Under the designed ladder (docs/battles/README.md, the brief) it carries the verdict, the clock, the own army with its qualities and the enemy's order of battle in outline, numbers included. The umpire never reads this file.

## Horse: General Ashdown

### Who you are

You command the White army, an army strong in horse: some eleven thousand two hundred men and thirty-six guns in eleven formations. Its foot is five brigades of two battalions of seven hundred men, each with two battalion guns: the Grenadier brigade under Birtwistle, picked men, quality 5 on a scale of 1 to 5; the First, Second and Third brigades under Cradock, Dimmock and Elphick, steady regiments of the line, quality 3; the Fourth brigade under Fothergill, new-raised regiments, quality 2. Its horse is five brigades of two regiments of four hundred: the Cuirassier brigade under Grimshaw, heavy horse, quality 4; the First and Second Dragoon brigades under Hollins and Ingleby, quality 3; the First and Second Hussar brigades under Jowett and Kershaw, light horse, quality 3. The Horse artillery under Lumb is two batteries of eight six-pounders, quality 3. Your headquarters stands behind the centre.

### How the army came here

The army is rested and fed. It stands drawn up across the Post road, its first line three and a half kilometres from Medlar Village: the First, Second and Third brigades from left to right, the First Hussar brigade on the left wing and the Second on the right; behind them the second line, the Fourth brigade on the left and the Grenadier brigade in column on the right, the two Dragoon brigades behind the wings; behind the centre the Cuirassier brigade and the Horse artillery limbered.

### What headquarters knows of the enemy

The enemy marches on Medlar Village from the other side of the plain. His army is unlike yours: the Green army, strong in foot, some thirteen thousand two hundred men and fifty-six guns in eleven formations. Eight brigades of foot, each of two battalions of seven hundred with two battalion guns apiece: a Grenadier brigade, quality 5, five brigades of the line, quality 3, and two of new-raised regiments, quality 2. Two brigades of horse, each of two regiments of four hundred: a Dragoon brigade and a Hussar brigade, quality 3. A Reserve artillery of three batteries of eight twelve-pounders, quality 3. How he has drawn them up and what he intends is not known.

### The ground

A flat, open plain, dry and firm, without hedge, wood, stream or rise. Medlar Village stands at its centre, on the crossroads of the Post road, which runs from the West exit behind you through the village to the East exit beyond it, and the Salt road, which crosses it there from the North exit on your left to the South exit on your right. The village, some four hundred metres across, gives good cover to those who hold it. Four farms stand in the open fields two kilometres from the village on the diagonals: Quince Farm on your left and Pippin Farm on your right, Damson Farm and Bullace Farm beyond it; they give no cover. Your way back is the Post road to the West exit.

### Your orders

Your orders are these: take and hold the crossroads at Medlar Village. The enemy marches on it from the other side under the same orders. The object is to win the day; a draw is worth half a win to each of you. The verdict, the same for both armies, is read by rule, not by an umpire's opinion: (1) An army that breaks loses. Broken: more than 40% of its men are in routed or destroyed bodies and stay so for 30 minutes together; if rallies bring the share under 40% within the half hour, the count starts again. The battle ends at that hour. If both armies are over 40% then, the less broken army wins, unless the two shares are within 10 points of each other (about a brigade of foot), when it is drawn. (2) Otherwise the side in possession of the village at four o'clock wins. Possession is read within musket range, 250 m, of the crossroads: the side with formed foot or guns there (steady or shaken, not in flight or routed) while the enemy has none; if neither has foot or guns there, the side with formed cavalry there while the enemy has none. Cavalry holds an empty village and holds it against cavalry, never against formed foot or guns, for in this game horse fire no volleys and dismount no dragoons. (3) Contested at four: play goes on, the village is read again every quarter hour, your staff tell you each reading, and the first clear possession wins. Still contested at six: a draw, unless one army is then over 40% broken and the other is not, in which case the broken army loses. (4) Nothing else counts. Casualties, ground elsewhere and the condition of your army for tomorrow decide nothing.

### The conventions of the day

Foot in column make some four or five kilometres in the hour, much less in line. Horse at the walk go somewhat faster than foot, twice as fast at the trot, but not for long. A battery needs a few minutes to unlimber and open fire, and as long again to limber up and move. The clock: the battle starts at 09:00; the end hour is 16:00; if the village is contested then, play goes on by quarter hours until 18:00 at most.

Its horse is two brigades of two regiments of four hundred: the Dragoon brigade under Wolstenholme, quality 3; the Hussar brigade under Yeadon, light horse, quality 3. The Reserve artillery under Brocklehurst is three batteries of eight twelve-pounders, quality 3. Your headquarters stands behind the centre.
