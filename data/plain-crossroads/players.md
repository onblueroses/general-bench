# Teasel Village: the players' brief

The persona's battle layer for each side (docs/player-prompt.md), built by tools/build_pack.py from tools/players_template.md with each side's names, so that the two sections differ only in names. Under the designed ladder (docs/battles/README.md, the brief) it carries the verdict, the clock, the qualities and the enemy's order of battle in outline, numbers included. The umpire never reads this file.

## Red: General Thursby

### Who you are

You command the Red army, some twelve thousand four hundred men and forty-four guns in eleven formations, each under its general. Its foot is seven brigades of two battalions of seven hundred men, each with two battalion guns: the Grenadier brigade under Calloway, picked men, quality 5 on a scale of 1 to 5; the First, Second, Third and Fourth brigades under Brandreth, Fenwick, Haddow and Pellow, steady regiments of the line, quality 3; the Fifth and Sixth brigades under Quarrie and Stannard, new-raised regiments, quality 2. Its horse is three brigades of two regiments of four hundred: the Cuirassier brigade under Ormerod, heavy horse, quality 4; the Dragoon brigade under Lattimer, quality 3; the Hussar brigade under Vowell, light horse, quality 3. The Reserve artillery under Kitto is two batteries of eight twelve-pounders, quality 3. Your headquarters stands behind the centre.

### How the army came here

The army is rested and fed. It stands drawn up across the High road, its first line three and a half kilometres from Teasel Village: the First, Second and Third brigades from left to right, the Hussar brigade on the left wing and the Dragoon brigade on the right; behind them the second line, the Fifth, Fourth and Sixth brigades, with the Cuirassier brigade behind the dragoons; behind the centre the Grenadier brigade in column and the Reserve artillery limbered.

### What headquarters knows of the enemy

The enemy marches on Teasel Village from the other side of the plain. His army is the same as yours, formation for formation: the Blue army, some twelve thousand four hundred men and forty-four guns; a Grenadier brigade of two battalions, quality 5; the First to the Fourth brigades of the line, quality 3; the Fifth and Sixth brigades of new-raised regiments, quality 2; each brigade of two battalions of seven hundred with two battalion guns apiece; a Cuirassier brigade, quality 4, a Dragoon brigade, quality 3, and a Hussar brigade, quality 3, each of two regiments of four hundred; and a Reserve artillery of two batteries of eight twelve-pounders, quality 3. How he has drawn them up and what he intends is not known.

### The ground

A flat, open plain, dry and firm, without hedge, wood, stream or rise. Teasel Village stands at its centre, on the crossroads of the High road, which runs from the West exit behind you through the village to the East exit beyond it, and the Drove road, which crosses it there from the North exit on your left to the South exit on your right. The village, some four hundred metres across, gives good cover to those who hold it. Four farms stand in the open fields two kilometres from the village on the diagonals: Mallow Farm on your left and Burdock Farm on your right, Vetch Farm and Yarrow Farm beyond it; they give no cover. Your way back is the High road to the West exit.

### Your orders

Your orders are these: take and hold the crossroads at Teasel Village. The enemy marches on it from the other side under the same orders. The object is to win the day; a draw is worth half a win to each of you. The verdict, the same for both armies, is read by rule, not by an umpire's opinion: (1) An army that breaks loses. Broken: more than 40% of its men are in routed or destroyed bodies and stay so for 30 minutes together; if rallies bring the share under 40% within the half hour, the count starts again. The battle ends at that hour. If both armies are over 40% then, the less broken army wins, unless the two shares are within 10 points of each other (about a brigade of foot), when it is drawn. (2) Otherwise the side in possession of the village at four o'clock wins. Possession is read within musket range, 250 m, of the crossroads: the side with formed foot or guns there (steady or shaken, not in flight or routed) while the enemy has none; if neither has foot or guns there, the side with formed cavalry there while the enemy has none. Cavalry holds an empty village and holds it against cavalry, never against formed foot or guns, for in this game horse fire no volleys and dismount no dragoons. (3) Contested at four: play goes on, the village is read again every quarter hour, your staff tell you each reading, and the first clear possession wins. Still contested at six: a draw, unless one army is then over 40% broken and the other is not, in which case the broken army loses. (4) Nothing else counts. Casualties, ground elsewhere and the condition of your army for tomorrow decide nothing.

### The conventions of the day

Foot in column make some four or five kilometres in the hour, much less in line. Horse at the walk go somewhat faster than foot, twice as fast at the trot, but not for long. A battery needs a few minutes to unlimber and open fire, and as long again to limber up and move. The clock: the battle starts at 09:00; the end hour is 16:00; if the village is contested then, play goes on by quarter hours until 18:00 at most.
