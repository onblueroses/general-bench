# Plover Village: the players' brief

The persona's battle layer for each side (docs/player-prompt.md), built by tools/build_pack.py from tools/players_template.md with each side's names, so that the two sections differ only in names. Under the designed ladder (docs/battles/README.md, the brief) it carries the verdict, the clock, the qualities and the enemy's order of battle in outline, numbers included. The umpire never reads this file.

## Red: General Gorringe

### Who you are

You command the Red army, some twelve thousand four hundred men and forty-four guns in eleven formations, each under its general. Its foot is seven brigades of two battalions of seven hundred men, each with two battalion guns: the Grenadier brigade under Pettifer, picked men, quality 5 on a scale of 1 to 5; the First, Second, Third and Fourth brigades under Ackroyd, Mawhood, Treloar and Bunce, steady regiments of the line, quality 3; the Fifth and Sixth brigades under Pinnock and Snelling, new-raised regiments, quality 2. Its horse is three brigades of two regiments of four hundred: the Cuirassier brigade under Hebditch, heavy horse, quality 4; the Dragoon brigade under Cundall, quality 3; the Hussar brigade under Lorimer, light horse, quality 3. The Reserve artillery under Venables is two batteries of eight twelve-pounders, quality 3. Your headquarters stands behind the centre.

### How the army came here

The army is rested and fed. It stands drawn up across the High road, its first line three and a half kilometres from Plover Village: the First, Second and Third brigades from left to right, the Hussar brigade on the left wing and the Dragoon brigade on the right; behind them the second line, the Fifth, Fourth and Sixth brigades, with the Cuirassier brigade behind the dragoons; behind the centre the Grenadier brigade in column and the Reserve artillery limbered.

### What headquarters knows of the enemy

The enemy marches on Plover Village from the other side of the brooks. His army is the same as yours, formation for formation and quality for quality: the Blue army, some twelve thousand four hundred men and forty-four guns in seven brigades of foot, three of horse and a reserve artillery. How he has drawn them up and what he intends is not known.

### The ground

Two brooks rise on a dry saddle half a kilometre either side of the village and run to the corners behind your left and beyond your right. Every arm crosses them only at a ford, slowly, two kilometres from the village: Curlew Ford and Curlew Mill on Curlew Brook, to your left; Godwit Ford and Godwit Mill on Godwit Brook, to your right. The village stands on the saddle and gives good cover; there the High road, from the West exit behind you to the East exit beyond, crosses the Withy road, from Curlew Ford on your side, and the Osier road, from Godwit Ford on his. Curlew lane leads past Wren Farm to Curlew Ford, the short way round his right; Godwit lane past Finch Farm to Godwit Ford, the long way round his left. On your left, a kilometre and a half from the village, Heron Ridge rises twenty-five metres; from its near end twelve-pounders reach the village and Curlew Ford. On your right Siskin Wood hides the way to Godwit Ford to within three hundred metres of the brook. His side is the same ground turned about: Bittern Ridge on his left, Linnet Wood on his right.

### Your orders

Your orders are these: take and hold the crossroads at Plover Village. The enemy marches on it from the other side under the same orders. The object is to win the day; a draw is worth half a win to each of you. The verdict, the same for both armies, is read by rule, not by an umpire's opinion: (1) An army that breaks loses. Broken: more than 40% of its men are in routed or destroyed bodies and stay so for 30 minutes together; if rallies bring the share under 40% within the half hour, the count starts again. The battle ends at that hour. If both armies are over 40% then, the less broken army wins, unless the two shares are within 10 points of each other (about a brigade of foot), when it is drawn. (2) Otherwise the side in possession of the village at four o'clock wins. Possession is read within musket range, 250 m, of the crossroads: the side with formed foot or guns there (steady or shaken, not in flight or routed) while the enemy has none; if neither has foot or guns there, the side with formed cavalry there while the enemy has none. Cavalry holds an empty village and holds it against cavalry, never against formed foot or guns, for in this game horse fire no volleys and dismount no dragoons. (3) Contested at four: play goes on, the village is read again every quarter hour, your staff tell you each reading, and the first clear possession wins. Still contested at six: a draw, unless one army is then over 40% broken and the other is not, in which case the broken army loses. (4) Nothing else counts. Casualties, ground elsewhere and the condition of your army for tomorrow decide nothing.

### The conventions of the day

Foot in column make some four or five kilometres in the hour, much less in line. Horse at the walk go somewhat faster than foot, twice as fast at the trot, but not for long. A battery needs a few minutes to unlimber and open fire, and as long again to limber up and move. The clock: the battle starts at 09:00; the end hour is 16:00; if the village is contested then, play goes on by quarter hours until 18:00 at most.
