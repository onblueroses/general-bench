# Kolín, 18 June 1757: objectives

Each side's objective as its commander would have put it at noon, from the historical record
(sources in docs/kolin-test-pack.md). The umpire judges the end of the day against these,
free-form. No victory points.

## Prussia: Frederick II

Daun's army stands on the heights south of the Kaiserstrasse between Chotzemitz and
Krzeczhorz, blocking the road to Kolín and threatening the siege of Prague. His left and his
front are too strong to be attacked. His right, at Krzeczhorz and the oak wood, hangs in the
air. The army will march east along the high road past his front, wheel to the right beyond
Krzeczhorz and roll up his right wing in successive attacks from the left, the vanguard under
Hülsen and Zieten leading, while the right wing of the infantry is refused and stays out of
reach of his batteries. The object of the day: turn Daun's right, dislodge him from the heights,
and have the road to Kolín open before nightfall, so that he can no longer relieve Prague.
Defeat that leaves the army intact is recoverable; the loss of the army is not.

## Austria: Daun

The army holds the heights from the Kamhajek to Krzeczhorz, across the road by which the
King must pass to reach Kolín, and it is the only army that can relieve Prague. Hold the
heights and the road, keep the right at Krzeczhorz and the oak wood firm with the reserve and
Nádasdy's horse, and let the Prussians break themselves on the position. Preserve the army
above all: do not let it be driven down the south slope into the ponds and swamps, and do not
leave the heights to pursue. Should the Prussian attack break, drive them off the field and
away from the road to Prague.

## Outcome (historical, for the record; not shown to commanders)

The Prussian attacks on the Austrian right and centre failed one after another between about
14:00 and 20:00; the Saxon and Austrian cavalry broke Hülsen's and Tresckow's infantry near
Krzeczhorz and the army retreated on Nimburg. Prussia lost about 13,770 men and 45 guns,
Austria about 8,100 to 9,000; Frederick raised the siege of Prague and left Bohemia.
